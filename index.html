01 — About the journal

A journal that publishes the evidence behind its own decisions.

PRISM is an open-access journal for research carried out by secondary-school students, across every scientific discipline. It is edited by students and overseen by faculty. Everything it publishes is free to read, and nothing costs anything to submit.

Student journals usually ask to be trusted on the strength of who is behind them. PRISM asks to be trusted on the strength of what it shows you. Every accepted paper is published together with its reviewer reports, the authors' response, and a decision letter signed by the faculty member who approved it. If a referee objected and the objection was answered, you can read both. If a limitation survived into the final version, it is stated in the paper and named in the decision letter.

That is the whole editorial argument. A reader does not have to take our word for the quality of a paper, because the record of how it was judged is attached to it.

The name is the method. A prism belongs to no single discipline; it takes one beam and separates it into its wavelengths. PRISM takes one question and lets several fields answer it — a result in ecology and a result in statistics belong in the same issue when both are sound, and a reader in one should be able to follow the other.

02 — Aims & scope

Deliberately broad in subject. Deliberately narrow in standard.

PRISM considers original research, replications, and critical reviews in any of six sections. Work that sits between two sections is welcome and will be reviewed by both.

One beam, six wavelengths — the sections below, in spectral order.

  • Physics & Astronomy

    Mechanics, optics, electronics, astronomy and observational projects, instrumentation built by the authors, computational physics.

  • Chemistry & Materials

    Synthesis and characterisation, electrochemistry, analytical methods, materials testing, green chemistry, kitchen- and classroom-scale experimental work done rigorously.

  • Biology & Health

    Molecular and cell biology, microbiology, plant and animal physiology, genetics, bioinformatics, public-health and epidemiological analysis of open data.

  • Earth & Environment

    Ecology and field surveys, water and air quality, climate data analysis, geology, conservation, agriculture, long-term local monitoring.

  • Computation & Mathematics

    Algorithms, machine learning, simulation, applied statistics, pure mathematics with complete proofs, software and tools built for other researchers.

  • Quantitative Social Science

    Survey and experimental work on human behaviour, economics, linguistics, psychology and education research — held to the same standards of design, consent and pre-specified analysis.

We do not claim that everything we publish is important. We claim that everything we publish can be checked.

The editorial position of PRISM

03 — Criteria for publication

Four questions every referee is asked.

We do not judge manuscripts on how impressive the topic sounds, how expensive the equipment was, or where the author goes to school. Referees answer these four questions and nothing else.

  1. I

    Is it sound?

    The design, controls, sample size and statistics support the claim being made — and support nothing stronger than that claim. Overreach in the abstract is a reason for revision, not rejection, provided the underlying work holds.

  2. II

    Is it honest?

    The manuscript states plainly what was already known before the study, what the study adds, what it does not show, and what went wrong. Negative and inconclusive results are publishable in PRISM; concealed ones are not.

  3. III

    Can it be checked?

    A competent reader with the deposited data, code and procedure could repeat the analysis and arrive at the same numbers. This is the criterion we are strictest about, because it is the one a student journal can enforce completely.

  4. IV

    Can it be read across fields?

    A student working in a different section can follow the question, the figures, and the limits of the result. Jargon that is load-bearing stays; jargon that is decoration goes.

05 — Peer review

Students do the reviewing. A faculty member signs the decision.

Every manuscript is read by at least two trained student reviewers and by one faculty reviewer, who writes and signs the decision letter. No paper is accepted on student review alone.

  1. 01

    Submission Author

    Any legible format, one PDF. No formatting requirements before acceptance. Preprints are welcome and posting one does not count as prior publication.

  2. 02

    Editorial screening Student editors

    Two student editors check scope, completeness, and whether the data and code statements are real. Decided within 10 working days. Manuscripts declined here get a written reason naming what was missing — never a form letter.

  3. 03

    Student peer review ≥ 2 reviewers

    At least two student reviewers from the relevant section, working from a published rubric mapped to the four criteria. Reviewers complete a short training module before their first assignment. Single-anonymised by default: reviewers see the authors, authors do not see reviewers unless a reviewer chooses to sign.

  4. 04

    Faculty review and decision Faculty

    A faculty reviewer reads the manuscript and the student reports, then writes the decision letter — stating which reviewer points are binding and which are optional — and signs it under their own name. This step cannot be skipped or delegated to a student editor.

  5. 05

    Revision Author

    One substantive round is normal. Revisions return to the original reviewers wherever possible. Authors respond point by point; that response is part of the published record.

  6. 06

    Publication Editorial office

    Article and full review record published together under CC BY 4.0, with a DOI issued at acceptance. Continuous publication: papers appear when they are ready and are gathered into the current issue.

Reports on rejected manuscripts are never published. The record exists to let readers judge accepted work, not to expose authors whose work was declined. Reviewers may sign their reports or stay anonymous; either way the report is published, and signed reviewers are credited by name in the article.

06 — For authors

What a submission must contain.

Formatting is not a barrier at PRISM — style and layout are handled after acceptance. These requirements are about substance, and a manuscript missing any of them is returned at screening.

Data availability Required
The data behind every figure and statistic must be deposited in an open repository (Zenodo, OSF, Figshare or a subject repository) with a DOI, before publication. Raw data where possible, processed data at minimum. "Available on request" is not an availability statement and is not accepted.
Code availability Required
Any script or notebook used for analysis, simulation or figure generation is deposited with a licence and a DOI. Spreadsheet analyses count: deposit the workbook with formulas intact.
Mentor contribution statement Required
Name every teacher, mentor, lab host or family member who contributed, and say exactly what they did — proposed the question, provided equipment, ran an instrument, wrote code, edited the text. This is the single most important integrity statement in a student journal, and referees are instructed to weigh the paper against it. A well-mentored project is not worth less. An undisclosed one is not publishable.
Prior knowledge and limitations
A paragraph stating what was known before the study, and a separate paragraph stating what the results do not establish. Both are required in the manuscript body, not only in a cover letter.
Methods detail
Sample sizes and how they were chosen, every exclusion and why, the exact statistical tests used, and the software and versions. Enough that a reader could repeat the procedure without contacting you.
Use of AI tools Disclose
Generative AI used for analysis, code, figures, translation or drafting must be disclosed in the methods, naming the tool and what it did. Using AI is permitted and disclosed use is never held against a manuscript. Undisclosed use is treated as a research-integrity matter. AI systems cannot be authors, because they cannot be accountable.
Authorship
Authors are the people who did the work and can defend it. Every author approves the final version and signs a contribution statement using the CRediT taxonomy. Adding a name for goodwill — a mentor, a supervisor, a friend — is not permitted.
Cost to authors None
PRISM charges nothing to submit, nothing to review, and nothing to publish. There are no article processing charges, no page charges, and no colour charges. The journal is run by volunteers and hosted by [host institution].

07 — Ethics & research integrity

Rules that exist because student research has its own risks.

Human participants
Any study involving people — including classroom surveys and online questionnaires — requires documented approval from a school ethics committee, IRB, or equivalent review body, plus informed consent. Where participants are under 18, parental or guardian consent is also required. Submissions must include the approval reference or an explicit statement of the exemption relied on.
Data about minors
Datasets containing information about minors must be de-identified before deposition, and the manuscript must describe how. Where de-identification would destroy the data's usefulness, deposit under controlled access and say so.
Animals and field work
Vertebrate work requires institutional approval and adherence to ARRIVE reporting guidelines. Field studies must state collection permits, protected-area permissions, and any species handled.
Laboratory safety
Work involving hazardous chemicals, biological agents, high voltages, lasers or pressure vessels must state the supervising adult and the facility. PRISM will not publish work that appears to have been carried out unsupervised where supervision was required, regardless of the quality of the result.
Originality screening
Every manuscript reaching review is checked with similarity-detection software, and figures are screened for duplication and manipulation. Detected issues are raised with the authors before any decision is made.
Competing interests
Declared by authors, reviewers and editors. No student reviews a manuscript from their own school; no faculty member decides on a manuscript from a student they teach or mentor. Declarations are published with the article.
Corrections and retractions
PRISM follows COPE guidance. The published version of record is never silently amended: corrections and retractions are separate, permanent, linked in both directions, and state plainly what was wrong. A retraction is not a judgment on the authors' character.
Appeals
One appeal per manuscript, in writing, decided by a faculty editor who had no part in the original decision. Appeals must address the reviewers' reasoning, not the outcome.
Preservation
All content is archived with [preservation service] so that it remains available if the journal ceases to operate — a real risk for any student-run publication, and one PRISM plans for rather than ignores.

08 — Editorial board

Who does what, and who is accountable.

PRISM is run by students. It is answerable through its faculty board, which holds the authority no student role holds: the power to accept a paper.

  • Editor-in-Chief Henry — a student. Sets editorial direction, chairs the editorial meeting, and is answerable for the journal's conduct. Cannot accept a manuscript alone.
  • Deputy Editors Aiden, Edward, Alvin — students. Run the submission queue, assign referees, and may sign decisions. Appoint and remove every role below their own.
  • Editorial Board Leo, Albert, Louis — advise on scope, propose referees, and read the whole queue. Advisory: they do not decide on manuscripts.
  • Reviewer Corps Jake, Finley, and every student who completes the training module. Reviewers work from a published rubric and are credited annually. Open to any student, including those who have not published. — [applications open]
  • Section Editors ×6 Students, one per section. Screen submissions, select and brief referees, and prepare the file that goes to a signing editor. — [applications open]
  • Faculty Editors Teachers and researchers who read the manuscript and the referee reports, then write and sign the decision letter under their own name. This is the step that makes a PRISM decision worth anything, and PRISM will not publish a paper without it. — [nominations open]
  • Statistics Reviewers Consulted whenever a central claim rests on a statistical test. Any editor or referee can request this review, and the request cannot be overruled.

Student editors serve one academic year with one renewal; faculty editors serve two years. Both terms are published, so that a reader in five years can tell who was responsible for a decision made today. Referees are never listed against a particular manuscript unless they choose to sign their report.

09 — Issues

One issue, one wavelength.

Each issue takes a single colour and keeps it across the cover, the site, and the typeset articles. The colours advance along the visible spectrum, beginning at the red end — so an issue's number and its colour are the same fact stated twice.

Click an issue to take its colour across the whole page.

Every wavelength is a real emission line. The four you see above are among the first spectral lines ever catalogued — which is a small way of saying that separating light into its parts is where a great deal of modern science began.
