Summary

This manuscript evaluates four micropeptide-source RNAs as predictors of disease-free interval in stage II/III colon adenocarcinoma using TCGA-COAD data. The prespecified four-gene panel does not meet the stated performance threshold and does not significantly outperform random four-gene panels. The manuscript therefore supports its primary negative conclusion.

An exploratory analysis identifies LINC00675 as the strongest individual marker, with a held-out C-index of approximately 0.70. The authors appropriately distinguish this exploratory finding from the prespecified panel analysis and further evaluate it using a matched single-gene null and a selection-aware correction.

The manuscript is clear about the distinction between prespecified and exploratory analyses and appropriately discusses several statistical limitations. I consider the study suitable for publication after the points below are addressed.

Major point 1 — Assess the proportional-hazards assumption

The analyses rely on Cox proportional-hazards models, but the manuscript states that proportional-hazards adequacy was not formally examined. This should be assessed, particularly for LINC00675.

The crossing Kaplan–Meier curves do not necessarily demonstrate violation of the proportional-hazards assumption for the continuous Cox model, but they make a formal diagnostic appropriate.

Please report a proportional-hazards diagnostic, such as a Schoenfeld-residual test, and briefly discuss its implications for interpretation of the Cox-model results.

Major point 2 — Clarify the selection-aware null

The single-gene null is an important addition because it compares LINC00675 with genes passing the same expression requirements.

However, the Methods should describe the best-of-four procedure in sufficient detail for independent reproduction. In particular, please specify:

  • how genes were sampled in each resampling iteration;
  • whether sampling was performed with or without replacement;
  • how ties were handled; and
  • how the selection-aware empirical probability was calculated.

The manuscript should also clarify that this procedure represents a selection-aware empirical calibration rather than an exact reconstruction of the biological candidate-selection process. This distinction is relevant because the four candidate genes may not be statistically independent.

Major point 3 — Clarify uncertainty around the LINC00675 C-index

The reported confidence interval around the LINC00675 C-index conditions on the fitted models and does not repeat marker selection or the complete model-development procedure.

Although this limitation is discussed later in the manuscript, it should also be stated where the confidence interval is first reported so that readers do not interpret it as representing total model-selection uncertainty.

A description such as “conditional bootstrap confidence interval given the fitted cross-validation models” would make the scope of the interval clearer.

Minor points

1. The expanded DFI cohort should be described as a sensitivity analysis rather than an independent validation cohort.

2. The distinction between RNA abundance and micropeptide abundance or function should remain explicit in the interpretation of the results.

3. Both random-panel comparisons should be reported: the analysis using all random panels and the analysis restricted to nondegenerate panels. These answer related but distinct questions.

4. The selection-aware p ≈ 0.042 should not be presented as strong independent evidence of a validated biomarker. Given the small number of DFI events and the exploratory origin of the finding, cautious interpretation is appropriate.

5. The reproducibility package would benefit from deposition in a persistent repository with a DOI or comparable permanent identifier.

Overall assessment

The strongest contribution of the manuscript is the prespecified negative result. The study defines a performance criterion in advance, evaluates the candidate panel against random alternatives, and reports that the proposed panel fails to satisfy the prespecified criterion.

The LINC00675 result is potentially interesting but remains exploratory. Its predictive performance, selection-aware null result, small event count, absence of independent validation, and lack of demonstrated incremental value beyond clinical variables all warrant cautious interpretation.

The requested revisions concern statistical clarification and reporting rather than a substantial redesign of the study. After these points are addressed, I would support publication.

What the revised version did

All three major points and four of the five minor points are addressed in the version now posted. The author did not carry out these revisions; the journal did, and the working paper says so on its own page.

Major 1. A Grambsch–Therneau correlation test on scaled Schoenfeld residuals (§11.1) finds no evidence against proportional hazards for any of the four transcripts: χ² = 0.49 for LINC00467, 0.00 for ELFN1-AS1, 1.13 for LINC00675/TMEM238L and 0.00 for HOXB-AS3. With 18 events the test has little power, which the section states; what it does settle is that the crossing in the median-split figure does not carry into the continuous model, which is the model actually fitted.

Major 2. §4.1 now states that the four genes are drawn without replacement, that a resample counts when its maximum is greater than or equal to the observed C-index, and that drawing with replacement instead moves the figure by less than the Monte Carlo error — 4.21% against 4.19%, with a standard error near 0.05 percentage points. The quantity is named a selection-aware empirical calibration rather than a multiplicity-corrected significance level, and the non-independence of the four candidates is stated as the reason.

Major 3. The interval is described as conditional on the fitted cross-validation models at its first appearance in the abstract, again in §7, and in a note beneath the single-marker table.

Minor points 1 to 4 were adopted, or were already satisfied and deliberately left unchanged. Minor point 5 is outstanding: the reproducibility package is still journal-hosted and carries no DOI, which is why the Checkable score of 4/5 stands.

One analysis was added that this version of the report did not ask for. An earlier draft of the report raised clinical covariate adjustment, and that analysis was run and kept (§11.2): adjusting for stage moves the LINC00675 coefficient from −0.5954 to −0.5950, an attenuation of 0.07%. It is reported alongside the reason it proves less than it appears — stage has a held-out C-index of 0.405 in this cohort, so surviving adjustment for it is close to free.
