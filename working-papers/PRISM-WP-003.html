Four micropeptide-source RNAs fail to predict colon cancer recurrence, and the one that appears to was chosen after the fact

A panel picked for mechanism rather than by screening, tested against a thousand random panels put through the same machinery — on eighteen recurrences.

Leo Lee1

Abstract

Transcripts once classified as long noncoding RNAs can encode functional peptides, and several of those peptides have documented roles in colorectal cancer. This study asked whether the source RNAs of four experimentally characterised micropeptides — LINC00467 (ASAP), ELFN1-AS1 (SMIMP), LINC00675/TMEM238L (FORCP) and HOXB-AS3 — form a useful recurrence panel in colon adenocarcinoma.

They do not. In 137 stage II/III TCGA-COAD patients with 18 disease-free interval (DFI) events, the four-transcript elastic-net Cox panel reached a held-out C-index of 0.609 (95% CI, 0.509–0.717) under five-by-three nested cross-validation repeated over five seeds, below the 0.65 development target set in advance. Against 1,000 random four-gene panels put through the identical procedure, it ranked at the 93.4th percentile, one-sided empirical p = 0.067. The prespecified comparison fails at its own threshold.

The individual transcripts are the more useful result. Three of the four discriminated at or below chance for primary DFI: 0.462 for LINC00467, 0.367 for ELFN1-AS1 and 0.515 for HOXB-AS3. Across the three cohorts and four transcripts, 7 of 12 estimates fall below 0.5. Only LINC00675 stood out, at 0.700 (95% CI, 0.583–0.819), and it was the sole transcript retained in the final penalised refit. That figure is the reason for this paper's existence and also the reason it cannot conclude anything: LINC00675 was selected for attention after the comparisons were seen, the cohort holds 18 events, its 25 outer-fold estimates run from 0.340 to 0.899, its all-stage overall-survival C-index is 0.502, and a post hoc median split did not separate DFI (log-rank p = 0.115). No random single-gene null was computed, so there is no calibrated reference for 0.700 at all.

We also report a feature of the comparator distribution that bears on the headline test. Of the 1,000 random panels, 264 (26.4%) returned a C-index of exactly 0.500 because the penalty shrank every coefficient to zero, leaving constant risk. Among the 736 panels that fitted anything, 9.0% matched or beat the candidate panel, a one-sided p of 0.091 rather than 0.067. The panel's rank is flattered by a comparator distribution that is a quarter inert, and the negative conclusion is correspondingly firmer.

The contribution here is a clean prespecified negative result, an empirical null that most biomarker papers of this size do not build, and an explicit account of why the one encouraging number is not yet evidence.

Keywords: colorectal adenocarcinoma; LINC00675; TMEM238L; FORCP; micropeptide-source RNA; disease-free interval; TCGA; negative result.

1. Micropeptide-source RNAs as candidate markers

Small open reading frames in transcripts historically classified as long noncoding RNAs can encode functional peptides. Ribosome-profiling atlases have expanded the catalogue of human translated regions, and proteomic studies have supplied direct evidence for subsets of noncanonical translation products 1,2,3. These approaches distinguish transcription, translation, peptide detection and biological function. Expression of a source RNA is therefore a tractable candidate biomarker, but it cannot by itself establish the abundance or the activity of the encoded peptide. Everything below concerns RNA.

Several experimentally characterised peptides motivate a colorectal cancer panel. LINC00467-encoded ASAP interacts with ATP synthase and promotes mitochondrial respiration and tumour growth 4. ELFN1-AS1-encoded SMIMP, identified by functional interrogation of noncanonical open reading frames, interacts with SMC1A and supports repression of cell-cycle inhibitors 5. LINC00675-encoded FORCP localises to the endoplasmic reticulum, suppresses proliferation and tumorigenicity, and supports apoptosis under endoplasmic-reticulum stress in well-differentiated colorectal models 6. The HOXB-AS3 peptide antagonises hnRNP A1-dependent pyruvate kinase splicing and metabolic reprogramming 7. LINC00266-1-encoded RBRP enhances IGF2BP1 recognition of m⁶A-modified transcripts 8.

Mechanistic plausibility is not prognostic performance. Established colon-cancer expression classifications and commercial recurrence assays were both developed against independent cohorts and assessed alongside conventional clinical factors 9,10, a standard no single-cohort analysis can meet. This study tested whether a panel chosen on mechanism rather than on a survival screen would discriminate recurrence in TCGA colon adenocarcinoma, and then asked which individual transcripts carried whatever discrimination appeared.

2. What was fixed in advance, and what was not

The distinction matters more here than the point estimates, so it is stated before the results.

Fixed before the outcome data were examined: the four candidate transcripts and their Ensembl identifiers; the primary endpoint (DFI) and primary cohort (stage II/III); the 0.65 C-index development target; the nested cross-validation design, its grids and its seeds; the 1,000-panel random comparator and its one-sided empirical probability; and the fold-support rules for time-dependent AUC.

Decided after results were seen, and therefore exploratory: the focus on LINC00675; every single-marker contrast; the paired panel-minus-marker difference; and the expression median split. These are labelled post hoc wherever they appear and are not corrected for multiplicity. Readers should treat the prespecified panel test as the result of this study and everything concerning LINC00675 as hypothesis generation.

3. Data and cohorts

Bulk expression came from the UCSC Xena TCGA-COAD STAR-Counts matrix 11. That matrix contains log₂(count+1) values, as its dataset metadata specifies; the filename must not be read as indicating untransformed counts. Counts were reconstructed as 2ˣ−1, retaining fractional values arising from upstream averaging across portions and aliquots. Clinical outcomes and staging came from Supplemental Table S1 of the TCGA Clinical Data Resource, restricted to COAD 12. DFI and overall survival used the supplied event indicators and time fields with no outcome relabelling. Times were held in days; displayed months use 365.25/12 days.

Four source RNAs were matched to Ensembl identifiers after version-suffix removal. LINC00266-1/RBRP was considered mechanistically but excluded before any model was fitted, because no stable Ensembl identifier could be resolved in this annotation; the exclusion was logged as missing annotation, never as zero expression. Gene-level abundance does not resolve peptide-encoding isoforms.

4. Statistical analysis

Counts per million used the library sum over the full expression matrix, followed by log₂(CPM+1). This transformation is sample-local and pools no information across patients. A transcript was measurable if CPM reached 1 in at least 20% of training tumours. Each training partition separately determined median imputation, removal of constant features, and mean/standard-deviation scaling. Missing annotation was never replaced with zero. No batch correction was applied across folds.

The panel used an elastic-net penalised Cox model over a penaliser grid of {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} and an L1-ratio grid of {0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 1.0}. Nested cross-validation used five outer and three inner folds stratified by event status, repeated with seeds 11, 23, 37, 53 and 71. Inner-fold comparable-pair-weighted concordance selected hyperparameters, with ties favouring stronger penalisation and then a larger L1 ratio. Standardised coefficients below 10⁻⁴ in absolute value were treated as zero and the baseline hazard recomputed. A separate full-cohort refit summarised retained coefficients and was never used to score held-out discrimination.

Harrell's C-index compared risks only within each held-out outer fold. Comparable-pair counts were aggregated across folds within a seed, and the five seed-specific estimates averaged. Cumulative/dynamic AUC used inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting with the censoring distribution estimated on the corresponding training set. A horizon was reported only if, in every outer fold, there were at least three held-out events by the horizon, five held-out observations beyond it, ten training observations beyond it, and training censoring survival of at least 0.05. A horizon failing these rules was reported as not estimable rather than averaged over the folds that happened to pass — a conservative reporting rule, not a claim that every alternative estimator is undefined.

For the random comparator, 1,000 panels of four distinct genes were drawn uniformly from the full annotation universe after excluding the four candidates, and each was put through the identical nested cross-validation, measurement gate, preprocessing and hyperparameter search. The same draws were reused across cohorts. Genes were not prefiltered on whole-cohort expression nor matched to the candidates by expression level. Percentiles used midranks; the one-sided empirical probability was (1 + #{C_random ≥ C_panel})/1001.

Each transcript was also fitted alone in an unpenalised continuous-expression Cox model on exactly the panel's outer partitions, with gate, imputation and scaling fitted on the corresponding training set. Reusing the folds permits paired comparison but does not correct the optimism of choosing the strongest marker after inspecting results.

Percentile 95% confidence intervals used 1,000 patient-cluster bootstrap replicates, each patient's multiplicity shared across all repeated held-out predictions. These intervals condition on the fitted models and training censoring distributions: model fitting, hyperparameter tuning and marker selection were not repeated inside the bootstrap, so the intervals understate total uncertainty.

5. The panel did not beat its own null

The primary panel reached a held-out C-index of 0.609 (95% CI, 0.509–0.717), below the 0.65 target fixed in advance. It ranked at the 93.4th percentile of the 1,000 random four-gene panels, one-sided empirical p = 0.067. The mean random-panel C-index was 0.502. All 1,000 random panels were retained in the comparison; two failed inner candidate fits in a single random panel were handled by invalidating that hyperparameter candidate rather than dropping the panel.

The same conclusion holds in both secondary cohorts, with larger probabilities: 0.134 for expanded DFI and 0.102 for all-stage OS. A mechanistically chosen panel that cannot separate itself from randomly drawn genes put through the same machinery has not demonstrated that the mechanism carries prognostic information in this dataset.

5.1 A quarter of the null distribution is inert

The null in Fig. 1 has a conspicuous spike at exactly 0.500. It is not a rendering artefact. Of the 1,000 random panels, 264 (26.4%) returned a C-index of exactly 0.500, because for those panels the selected penalty shrank every coefficient to zero, leaving a constant risk score and therefore, by definition, a concordance of one half. These panels are not weak competitors; they are models that declined to fit.

This matters for the headline comparison. The published probability treats all 1,000 draws as comparators, giving 66 at or above the panel and p = 0.067. Restricted to the 736 panels that fitted anything at all, 66 of 736 (9.0%) match or beat the candidate panel, a one-sided probability of 0.091. The candidate panel's rank is flattered by a comparator distribution that is a quarter inert.

Both figures exceed 0.05 and the prespecified conclusion is unchanged, so this does not rescue or reverse anything. It is reported because the percentile is the paper's headline comparison and a reader is entitled to know that roughly a quarter of the distribution it is measured against consists of null models in the strict sense. The effect works against the panel, which is why the negative result should be read as firmer rather than more fragile.

6. What the elastic net kept

Only LINC00675/TMEM238L survived the final full-cohort refit, at penaliser 0.1 and L1 ratio 0.5. That final model must be distinguished from the models that produced the held-out predictions: across the 25 outer folds, LINC00675 was retained in 23, HOXB-AS3 in 18, LINC00467 in 16 and ELFN1-AS1 in 12. All four passed the measurement gate in every fold. The panel's C-index therefore describes a collection of fold-specific penalised models, not repeated fitting of one fixed model.

For primary DFI, three of four transcripts sat at or below chance. Across all twelve transcript-by-cohort combinations, seven estimates fall below 0.5, and LINC00467 is below 0.5 in all three cohorts. Choosing transcripts because their peptides have documented colorectal biology did not, in this dataset, yield transcripts that carry prognostic signal.

Against that background, LINC00675's 0.700 for primary DFI is the single exception among four mechanistically motivated candidates. The paired panel-minus-LINC00675 difference was −0.092 (95% CI, −0.192 to −0.007), conditional on the fitted models: the four-transcript strategy was worse than its own best component. That contrast is post hoc, uncorrected for multiplicity, and conditional on models the bootstrap did not refit.

8. Stage, endpoint and horizon support

In the 181-patient expanded DFI cohort with 21 events, LINC00675 reached 0.691 (95% CI, 0.571–0.802) against a panel C-index of 0.593. This cohort contains the primary cohort and is a stage-sensitivity analysis, not independent replication.

The endpoint contrast is the sharpest internal check available, and LINC00675 does not survive it. In the 428-patient OS cohort with 93 deaths — more than five times the primary cohort's events — LINC00675 achieved 0.502 (95% CI, 0.435–0.563), indistinguishable from chance. ELFN1-AS1, which was the worst DFI marker at 0.367, became the best OS marker at 0.588. A marker ordering that inverts between endpoints in the same resource is what one expects from noise, though cohort composition, mortality and treatment also differ between the two analyses and cannot be separated here.

9. The median split

The primary-cohort expression median was 1.14059 on the log₂(CPM+1) scale. The low-expression group held 69 patients and 12 DFI events; the high-expression group held 68 patients and 6 events. The unadjusted log-rank comparison was not significant (p = 0.115), and the curves cross during sparse late follow-up.

This cutoff was computed on the whole cohort for display only and never entered Cox fitting or cross-validated scoring. A dichotomised whole-cohort comparison and continuous-expression held-out discrimination answer different questions, and failing the first does not invalidate the second. But a reader looking for confirmation that LINC00675 separates recurrence will not find it here either.

10. The comparison that is missing

The decisive comparison was never run, and naming it precisely is the most useful thing this paper can do for whoever continues the work.

The panel has a null: 1,000 random four-gene panels through the same pipeline. LINC00675 has none. No random single-gene distribution was computed, so there is no calibrated answer to the only question that matters about 0.700: how often does a single gene drawn at random from roughly sixty thousand, fitted as an unpenalised Cox model on 137 patients with 18 events using these exact folds, reach a held-out C-index of 0.700?

Nothing in this study answers that. The panel's 93.4th percentile cannot be transferred to a single marker — it was computed for four-gene panels. The bootstrap interval of 0.583–0.819 excludes 0.5, but it conditions on a model chosen after the comparisons were seen and does not include selection uncertainty. The 25 outer-fold estimates run from 0.340 to 0.899, with an unweighted mean of 0.685 ± 0.158 (SD), a median of 0.706 and an interquartile range of 0.600 to 0.809. With 18 events, a fold-level range of more than half the available scale is what sampling noise alone can produce.

With three of four mechanistically chosen transcripts at or below chance, the prior that the fourth is real should be modest. The single-gene null is computationally identical to the panel null already built — draw genes one at a time instead of four, reuse the same folds, fit the same unpenalised model — and it would convert 0.700 from an uncalibrated number into either a signal or a coincidence. Until it exists, 0.700 should be read as the maximum of four correlated draws, which is exactly what it is.

11. What this study establishes

A panel of four micropeptide-source RNAs, chosen on documented peptide biology rather than by screening, does not predict disease-free interval in TCGA colon adenocarcinoma well enough to pursue. It missed a target set in advance and did not separate from randomly drawn panels under an identical procedure, in the primary cohort and in both secondary ones. That is a clean prespecified negative result, and it is the finding of this paper.

Three further things follow, none of them a biomarker claim. First, mechanistic selection did not concentrate prognostic signal: most of these transcripts discriminated at or below chance. Second, combining four transcripts was worse than using the best one, which identifies a failed strategy without identifying its statistical cause — the final refit kept only LINC00675, but the held-out predictions came from fold-specific models that often retained others, and feature inclusion, fitted direction and risk ties are all plausible contributors. Third, LINC00675 is a hypothesis worth the cost of one external test, on the strength of a documented differentiation-associated mechanism 6 rather than on the strength of 0.700.

What this study does not establish is that LINC00675 predicts recurrence. It was chosen after the fact, rests on 18 events, has no single-gene null, shows no signal for overall survival, and does not separate recurrence on a median split. Bulk abundance of this transcript could track epithelial differentiation or tumour cell fraction with no change in peptide abundance at all. RNA is not peptide: no immunohistochemistry or targeted mass spectrometry was performed, and nothing here can establish a FORCP-mediated mechanism.

12. Limitations

Eighteen events in the primary cohort is the limitation from which most others follow: unstable fold estimates, failed horizon support, and wide intervals. Excluding 168 of 305 eligible patients for missing DFI status may also introduce selection bias of unknown direction.

The bootstrap intervals condition on already-fitted cross-validation models and do not capture variability from model fitting, hyperparameter selection or post hoc marker choice. The repeated folds overlap and are not independent replications. Proportional-hazards adequacy was not assessed. Stage, age, treatment and mismatch-repair status were not added as covariates, so incremental value beyond clinical risk was never tested — and for a prognostic marker that is the question that decides clinical usefulness.

No external validation was performed. The expanded DFI cohort overlaps the primary cohort and the OS analysis draws on the same resource, so neither is independent. The random null consists of four-gene panels drawn without expression matching; it contextualises the panel and provides neither a single-marker test nor a substitute for validation.

Gene-level measurement cannot distinguish coding isoforms, translation efficiency or protein turnover, and the historical lncRNA label should not be read as a contemporary coding classification for TMEM238L. DFI is used exactly as supplied by the Clinical Data Resource and is not interchangeable with similarly named endpoints 12.

TCGA's racial imbalance is documented 13. In these cohorts White patients were a majority among those with recorded race, and race was unavailable for many. Generalisability across populations and care settings is untested, and the study is confined to colon adenocarcinoma; nothing here extends to rectal cancer.

13. What would settle it

Three things, in order of cost.

The random single-gene null described in §10 costs one rerun of existing code and would calibrate 0.700 against the only relevant reference. It should be done before any external data are sought, because a result at the 60th percentile of random single genes would end the line of enquiry cheaply.

If that null is passed, a locked external test of LINC00675 alone is next. GSE39582 is a practical candidate only if its array probes measure TMEM238L adequately and uniquely; probe mapping, preprocessing, outcome definition and the continuous-marker model must all be fixed before any outcome is examined.

The decisive result is neither of those in isolation. It is whether LINC00675 adds reproducible information beyond stage, mismatch-repair status, age and treatment. Reproducing an unadjusted association would leave the clinical question exactly where it is now.
