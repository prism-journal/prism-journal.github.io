Working paper · peer reviewed · Quantitative Social Science

EconForge as a Reproducible Python Toolkit for Steady State Computation in RBC and New Keynesian Models

Finley Dou · Shanghai High School International Division

PRISM-WP-004 · 28 October 2026 · CC BY 4.0 · PDF (21 pages) · Review record

Abstract

Numerical economics requires more than finding a vector that an optimizer calls successful. Researchers must identify the equations being solved, preserve their economic interpretation, and retain enough evidence to check the result. This paper examines EconForge 0.1.0a1 as a transparent workflow for deterministic steady-state computation. The study reconstructs the released real business cycle and New Keynesian benchmarks, derives their analytical steady states, and traces model parsing, scalar residual evaluation, automatic differentiation, trust-region least squares, and fresh residual verification. All five released model and starting-point configurations are reproduced and compared with separately evaluated analytical references. The results support the reproducibility of these particular computations and illustrate why solver termination, residual acceptance, and economic validity must remain separate. The evidence does not establish global convergence, arbitrary-model correctness, stochastic equilibrium validity, or cross-platform certification.
